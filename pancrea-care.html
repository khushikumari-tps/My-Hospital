Functions of the Pancreas

A flat organ tucked behind the stomach, roughly the length of your hand. It does two jobs at once: it makes the enzymes that break down what you eat, and it makes the insulin that controls your blood sugar.

When it stops doing either well, the symptoms are vague at first — bloating, back pain, weight that falls off without explanation. That vagueness is why pancreatic disease is so often found late, and why we built a programme around it.

Related Disease

The conditions we see most often in this programme. Each links through to how we treat it.

Pancreatitis

Sudden or long-standing inflammation of the pancreas. Severe upper abdominal pain that bores through to the back is the classic sign.

How we treat it

Pancreatic Cancer

Often silent until it obstructs the bile duct or causes weight loss. Early referral is what decides whether surgery is still an option.

How we treat it

Cysts & Pseudocysts

Fluid collections that follow inflammation or appear on their own. Some need watching, some need draining, a few need removing.

How we treat it

Gallstone & Bile Duct Disease

The commonest trigger for acute pancreatitis in India. Treating the stones is part of treating the pancreas.

How we treat it

Pancreatogenic Diabetes

Diabetes caused by a damaged pancreas behaves differently from type 2. It needs a different plan, not a standard one.

How we manage it

Exocrine Insufficiency

When enzyme output drops, food passes through undigested — greasy stools, weight loss, fatigue. Enzyme replacement changes it.

How we manage it

What We Do

From the first scan to the follow-up call after you are home — the path is the same for every patient in the programme.

1

Diagnose Properly, Once

CT, MRI, endoscopic ultrasound and guided sampling in one place, so you are not sent between centres collecting reports. Existing scans are reviewed rather than repeated where they are adequate.

2

Decide as a Team

Surgery, gastroenterology, oncology, radiology and nutrition review the case together before a plan is offered. You are told what the options are, including the option of waiting.

3

Operate Where It Helps

Whipple's procedure, distal pancreatectomy, drainage procedures and complex reoperations — done by a team that performs this work regularly, not occasionally.

4

Feed and Rebuild

Nutrition is not an afterthought in pancreatic disease. Enzyme support, diet progression and blood sugar control are planned before surgery and carried through recovery.

5

Follow Up Without the Journey

Routine follow-up by phone or video for outstation patients, with appointments clustered so you travel once instead of four times.
