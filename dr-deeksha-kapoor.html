Real-World Evidence of Porto-Mesenteric Vein Resections with Pancreatectomy (PRIME Study)

Annals of Surgical Oncology · Kapoor D, Bhandare MS, Sharma A, et al. · July 5, 2025

Summary
Original Article
  1. The PRIME Study is a retrospective multicenter analysis from 11 high-volume Indian centers (2015–2023) evaluating outcomes of porto-mesenteric vein resection (PVR) performed with pancreatectomy and creating predictive nomograms for 90-day postoperative mortality (POM) and major complications (MC).
  2. In 389 included patients, MC (Clavien–Dindo ≥3a) occurred in 32.6%, 30-day mortality was 2.8%, and 90-day POM was 6.4%.
  3. Independent predictors of 90-day POM were Charlson comorbidity index >4, preoperative biliary drainage, preoperative radiotherapy, segmental PVR, and additional organ resection.
  4. For MCs, the key independent predictors were ASA grade 3/4, preoperative radiotherapy, and additional organ resection, and the study reports nomogram performance of AUC 0.757 for POM (cutoff >1.79) and AUC 0.669 for MC (cutoff >0.678).
  5. Among 263 patients with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, median overall survival was 25.01 months and disease-free survival was 16.72 months, with worse outcomes linked to perineural invasion/segmental PVR/margin positivity and improved survival associated with completing planned multimodality treatment.

ASO Author Reflections: Predictive Clinical Nomograms for Postoperative Outcomes Following Porto-Mesenteric Vein Resection

Annals of Surgical Oncology · Shrikhande SV, Kapoor D, Chaudhari VA, Bhandare MS · July 12, 2025

Summary
Author Reflections
  1. 1. This “Author Reflections” piece explains how pancreatic surgery with porto-mesenteric vein resection (VR) evolved from early skepticism to wider acceptance as outcomes improved and systemic therapy advanced.
  2. 2. The PRIME Study reported real-world, multicentre Indian data across 11 experienced centres over 8 years, totaling 389 VR-with-pancreatectomy cases.
  3. 3. Reported outcomes were comparable to global literature, with 30-day postoperative mortality of 2.8%, 90-day mortality of 6.4%, and major complications of 32.6%.
  4. 4. Key predictors of worse postoperative outcomes included higher comorbidity burden, preoperative biliary drainage, preoperative radiation, segmental VR, and additional organ resection (with segmental VR linked to higher failure-to-rescue).
  5. 5. The authors argue that predictive nomograms integrating clinical and radiologic variables can improve patient selection and personalized risk stratification, and future progress will likely be driven by “biological resectability,” big data, and machine-learning–enabled precision surgery.

World Journal of Surgery · Uppal M, Mishra DS, Suman S, Kapoor D · Sep 26, 2025

Summary
Original Article
  1. 1. This study examines perceived gender bias and career disparities among gastrointestinal (GI) and hepato-pancreato-biliary (HPB) surgeons in India, highlighting how workplace culture and institutional factors can affect training, advancement, and leadership representation.
  2. 2. Using a nationwide, cross-sectional online questionnaire distributed via the IASG database and messaging/email forums (open for 8 weeks in Nov–Dec 2024), the authors assessed demographics, work environment, research, leadership, mentorship, and personal life; 1425 surgeons were contacted.
  3. 3. Among 190 respondents (26.8% female), female surgeons were younger and earlier in career stage, and they reported higher perceived differential treatment—especially from senior faculty and patients—despite comparable salaries.
  4. 4. Female surgeons also felt they had fewer advancement opportunities, were underrepresented in leadership/mentorship networks, reported greater work–life balance strain (including family planning and maternity leave concerns), and were less likely to hold editorial board or clinical leadership roles (even where publication counts were similar).
  5. 5. The authors conclude that meaningful gender-equity progress in Indian GI/HPB surgery will require targeted interventions—particularly structured mentorship and institutional/policy reforms—to improve advancement pathways, leadership representation, and workforce inclusivity.

Surgical Management of Postcholecystectomy Strasberg Type E4 Bile Duct Injuries

World Journal of Surgery · Kapoor D, Perwaiz A, Singh A, Yadav A, Chaudhary A · March 12, 2025

Summary
Original Article
  1. 1. This original research reports a retrospective observational experience (Oct 2003–Dec 2020) focused specifically on severe Strasberg type E4 postcholecystectomy bile duct injuries, where the right and left hepatic ducts are separated at the hilum and outcomes after standard repair are often poor.
  2. 2. Among 119 surgically managed bile duct injury patients, 16 (13.4%) had E4 injuries, most presenting with an external biliary fistula, and many had associated cholangitis and/or vascular injury, with repair typically delayed until sepsis/inflammation settled and nutrition was optimized.
  3. 3. In the earlier period (2003–2010), Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy (HJ) was the preferred index operation, but primary patency was lost in 9/10 patients (often followed by cholangitis/stricture) leading to frequent radiologic interventions, redo surgery, hepatectomy, and even liver transplantation in one case.
  4. 4. From 2010 onward, the authors increasingly performed upfront right hepatectomy with left-duct HJ for E4 injuries, reporting sustained patency without endoscopic/radiologic intervention at a median follow-up around 40 months, and better short-term morbidity compared with hepatectomy performed later as a redo after failed HJ (less transfusion, fewer major complications, and shorter hospital stay).
  5. 5. They conclude that HJ alone in E4 injuries frequently yields poor long-term results and that, in selected patients at specialized centres, an upfront right hepatectomy with left-duct anastomosis may provide a more durable solution.

Hand grip strength predicts major complications following pancreaticoduodenectomy: A prospective observational study

Annals of Hepatobiliary Pancreatic Surgery · P L, Kapoor D, Singh A, Perwaiz A, Chaudhary A · 2025

Summary
Observational Study
  1. 1. This prospective observational study (June 2021–March 2023) assessed whether the Modified Frailty Index (mFI) and preoperative hand grip strength (HGS) can predict outcomes after pancreaticoduodenectomy (PD) in 180 consecutive patients.
  2. 2. Major complications (Clavien–Dindo grade ≥3) occurred in 10.5% of patients, and 90-day mortality was 3.3%.
  3. 3. Weak HGS was much more common in patients who developed major complications (14/19) than in those who did not (28/161), whereas high mFI did not reach statistical significance.
  4. 4. On multivariate analysis, weak HGS independently predicted major complications (adjusted odds ratio 11.52) and was also linked with longer hospital stay, higher 90-day readmission, and more overall complications.
  5. 5. The authors conclude that a simple preoperative HGS test is a practical bedside tool to identify functional sarcopenia and improve risk stratification and counseling before PD.

Validation and Optimisation of the ISGPS Risk Classification for Postoperative Pancreatic Fistula after Pancreatoduodenectomy

Annals of Surgery · Kapoor D, Desiraju Y, Chaudhari VA, Bagwan AI, Chopde A, Namachivayam A, Bhandare MS, Shrikhande SV · Aug 13, 2024

Summary
Original Article
  1. 1. This retrospective analysis of 1422 consecutive pancreatoduodenectomies (2014–2023) externally validated the ISGPS two-factor, four-tier “high-risk pancreas” classification for predicting clinically relevant postoperative pancreatic fistula (CRPF), and specifically tested its performance in periampullary tumours (P-amps).
  2. 2. The overall CRPF rate was 22.2%, with a higher CRPF rate in P-amps (25.8%), reflecting the softer-gland biology typical of periampullary disease.
  3. 3. The original ISGPS model showed only moderate discrimination overall (AUC ~0.63) and performed worse in P-amps (AUC ~0.61), with calibration issues at higher predicted risks.
  4. 4. In multivariable analysis for P-amps, soft pancreas, pancreatic duct diameter ≤3 mm, and BMI ≥23 kg/m² were independent predictors of CRPF, and these variables were used to optimize the scoring system.
  5. 5. The adjusted P-amp–specific ISGPS score improved predictive performance (AUC ~0.67 in both training and internal test sets), and the authors conclude that site-specific risk models may be more accurate, though larger multicentre validation is still needed.

Primary Aorto-duodenal Fistula: A Case Report and Review of Literature

Surgery Case Reports · Gupta A, Kapoor D, Vats R, Bukhari SN, Goel D · Dec 2024

Summary
Case Report
  1. 1. Primary aorto-duodenal fistula (PADF) is a rare aortoenteric fistula between the abdominal aorta and the duodenum, typically requiring urgent diagnosis (UGIE/CT angiography) and prompt surgical or endovascular repair.
  2. 2. The report describes a 64-year-old man who presented with bleeding per rectum and hypovolemic shock, with a background history of multiple myeloma on thalidomide maintenance therapy.
  3. 3. After initial ICU resuscitation, he developed a major rebleed on day 3 with shock and cardiac arrest; bedside UGIE showed blood in the stomach/small bowel and a large third-part duodenal ulcer with transmitted pulsation but no active bleeding.
  4. 4. CT aortic angiography demonstrated a large partially thrombosed infrarenal aortic aneurysm with a likely fistulous communication to the third part of the duodenum, and the patient underwent total endovascular aneurysmal repair (TEVAR) due to high operative risk.
  5. 5. Following TEVAR, bleeding was controlled and the patient recovered without further inotropic support, ultimately improving clinically and being discharged on post-procedure day 11, supporting endovascular repair as a viable option in high-risk PADF patients.

Effect of Multimodal Prehabilitation on Muscle Mass in Rectal Cancer Patients Receiving Neoadjuvant Treatment

Indian Journal of Surgical Oncology · Yadav Y, Gupta A, Singh A, Kapoor D, et al. · July 2, 2024

Summary
Original Article
  1. 1. This prospective observational study (June 2021–March 2023) evaluated whether a multimodal prehabilitation program improves muscle mass and postoperative outcomes in locally advanced, resectable rectal cancer patients receiving long-course neoadjuvant therapy, compared with a historical cohort without prehabilitation.
  2. 2. The intervention was a ≥6-week, home-based protocol combining self-supervised exercise, nutritional counselling, smoking/alcohol cessation support, and psychological counselling during neoadjuvant treatment.
  3. 3. Muscle mass was assessed radiologically and, among 100 patients (44 intervention; 56 historical), the prehabilitation group showed a mean percentage increase in muscle mass while the historical cohort showed a mean decrease.
  4. 4. Improved muscle mass was significantly associated with earlier stoma function, earlier tolerance of soft diet, and fewer surgical site infections, with overall complications also lower in the prehabilitation group (29.5% vs 46.4%).
  5. 5. The authors conclude that multimodal prehabilitation can improve physiological status and may translate into better postoperative recovery, recommending it be started from the first outpatient visit and incorporated into management guidelines.

Journal of Gastrointestinal Cancers · Pandrowala SA, Kapoor D, Kunte A, et al. · June 14, 2024

Summary
Original Article
