Liver

Understanding non-cancerous liver diseases

The liver detoxifies, produces bile and stores energy. Many conditions that affect it are benign — non-cancerous, not immediately threatening, and manageable when found early.

What are benign liver diseases?

These are conditions affecting the liver without cancer or immediate risk of liver failure. They may involve structural or functional change, but are generally treatable and do not progress to cancer. Common types include:

  • Fatty liver disease (NAFLD / NASH).
  • Liver cysts.
  • Hepatic haemangiomas.
  • Liver adenomas.
  • Benign biliary lesions.
  • Haemochromatosis.

What is fatty liver disease (NAFLD)?

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease occurs when fat accumulates in liver cells without significant alcohol consumption. It is commonly linked to obesity, type 2 diabetes and poor diet. Non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) is the more severe form, involving inflammation and tissue damage.

Early management rests on lifestyle: weight loss, dietary improvement and increased physical activity, with medication reserved for complications.

What are liver cysts, and how are they treated?

Liver cysts are fluid-filled sacs, usually benign and asymptomatic, and often found incidentally during imaging for something else. Two main types exist:

  • Simple cysts: the most common, typically causing no problems.
  • Polycystic liver disease: a genetic condition producing multiple cysts.

Treatment depends on size and symptoms. Large or symptomatic cysts may be drained or removed surgically, but most need only regular monitoring.

What are hepatic haemangiomas?

Hepatic haemangiomas are the most common benign liver tumour, made up of clusters of blood vessels. They are usually asymptomatic and found incidentally, and typically require no treatment unless they cause pain or bleeding. Surgical removal becomes necessary only when complications arise.

What is a liver adenoma, and who is at risk?

Liver adenomas are benign tumours, more common in women, particularly those using oral contraceptives or anabolic steroids. Though generally harmless, they can rupture, bleed, or rarely turn malignant. Most are asymptomatic and found incidentally; large or symptomatic tumours may need surgical removal, while small ones are monitored.

Can benign liver disease cause symptoms?

Many cause no early symptoms at all. When symptoms do appear they include:

  • Abdominal pain, especially upper right.
  • Fatigue.
  • Nausea or loss of appetite.
  • Jaundice — yellowing of skin and eyes, and rare.
  • Itchy skin.

How are they diagnosed?

  • Physical examination and medical history.
  • Blood tests to assess liver function.
  • Imaging — ultrasound, CT or MRI.
  • Liver biopsy in selected cases.

What are the treatment options?

  • Lifestyle change: weight loss, diet and exercise for NAFLD and NASH.
  • Medication for underlying conditions.
  • Regular monitoring for stable disease.
  • Surgery for large or symptomatic adenomas, cysts or haemangiomas.

How can I reduce my risk?

  • Maintain a healthy weight.
  • Avoid excessive alcohol.
  • Eat a balanced diet with fruit, vegetables and healthy fats.
  • Get vaccinated against hepatitis A and B.
  • Manage diabetes, cholesterol and blood pressure.

The outlook is generally excellent. Most benign liver conditions cause no long-term damage and respond well to lifestyle change or minor intervention.
